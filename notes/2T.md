# \(2T\): representation theory of the Hurwitz units

\(2T\) is the unit group of the Hurwitz order. Its representation theory is classical and sits under QGA’s discrete symmetries. It does **not** solve OP1.

QGA sources: Ch. 1 §1.4 (24 units = binary tetrahedral group); Ch. 3 \(\Lambda_{0}=\mathcal{H}\cap S^{3}\); Ch. 4 left/right gauge. Fiber of this \(h\): `notes/hopf.md`. Incidence still open: `notes/op1.md`. Conjugation vs Hopf: `notes/rotations.md`.

Claim label: **Theorem** (finite subgroups of \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\), McKay). A \(2T\)-invariant graph on \(\Lambda_{0}\) is only the cheap half of OP1 (**Model** candidate until Farey reduction is proved).

## The group, three ways

\[
2T
\;\cong\;
\mathcal{H}^{\times}
\;\cong\;
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{3})
\;\subset\;
\mathrm{SU}(2)
\;\cong\;
S^{3}.
\]

Order 24. Center \(\{\pm 1\}\). Quotient \(2T/\{\pm 1\}\cong A_{4}\cong T\subset SO(3)\). The extension

\[
1\to\{\pm 1\}\to 2T\to A_{4}\to 1
\]

does not split: \(2T\) has no subgroup of index 2. It does split the other way,

\[
2T \;=\; Q_{8}\rtimes C_{3},
\]

with \(Q_{8}=\{\pm 1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}\) normal of index 3 and \(C_{3}=\langle\omega\rangle\),

\[
\omega=-\tfrac12(1+i+j+k),
\]

acting by \(\omega i\omega^{-1}=j\), \(\omega j\omega^{-1}=k\), \(\omega k\omega^{-1}=i\).

Presentation (binary polyhedral \(\langle 2,3,3\rangle\)):

\[
\langle R,S\mid R^{3}=S^{3}=(RS)^{2}\rangle
\]

with that common value equal to the central \(-1\).

Elements by order, as Hurwitz units:

| Order | Count | Units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | \(1\) |
| 2 | 1 | \(-1\) |
| 4 | 6 | \(\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\) |
| 3 | 8 | \(\tfrac12(-1\pm i\pm j\pm k)\) |
| 6 | 8 | \(\tfrac12(+1\pm i\pm j\pm k)\) |

Seven conjugacy classes (sizes \(1,1,6,4,4,4,4\)), so seven irreps over \(\mathbb{C}\). The only nontrivial normal subgroups are \(\{\pm 1\}\) and \(Q_{8}\).

The convex hull of \(\Lambda_{0}\) is the 24-cell. That is the regular 4-polytope whose vertices are these units. Lattice, edges, and why that 1-skeleton is not the Hopf-fiber graph: `notes/hurwitz.md`.

## Irreps over \(\mathbb{C}\)

Dimensions \(1+1+1+3+2+2+2=24=\lvert 2T\rvert\). Split by the value on the center: \(\chi(-1)=\chi(1)\) means the rep factors through \(A_{4}\); \(\chi(-1)=-\chi(1)\) means it is spinorial (does not descend to \(SO(3)\)).

| Rep | Dim | \(\chi(-1)\) | Descends to \(A_{4}\)? | Geometric name |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(\rho_{1}\) | 1 | \(+1\) | yes | trivial |
| \(\rho_{2},\rho_{2}^{\ast}\) | 1 | \(+1\) | yes | the two nontrivial characters of \(2T/Q_{8}\cong C_{3}\) |
| \(\rho_{3}\) | 3 | \(+3\) | yes | conjugation on \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}\) |
| \(\rho_{5}\) | 2 | \(-2\) | no | defining: \(2T\hookrightarrow\mathrm{SU}(2)\) |
| \(\rho_{4},\rho_{4}^{\ast}\) | 2 | \(-2\) | no | defining twisted by \(\rho_{2},\rho_{2}^{\ast}\) |

Check sum of squares: \(1+1+1+9+4+4+4=24\).

On the class of order-4 units \(\{\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}\):

- \(\rho_{3}\) has character \(-1\) (a \(180^{\circ}\) rotation in \(\mathbb{R}^{3}\) has trace \(-1\));
- every 2-dimensional spinor has character \(0\) (a matrix in \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) for \(i\) is conjugate to \(\mathrm{diag}(i,-i)\)).

On order-3 units, the defining \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) character is \(-1\); on order-6 units it is \(+1\). The two companion 2-dimensional irreps replace those values by \(\pm\zeta_{3},\pm\zeta_{3}^{2}\).

Over \(\mathbb{R}\) the complex-conjugate pairs glue: real irreps of dimensions \(1\), \(2\) (\(\rho_{2}\oplus\rho_{2}^{\ast}\)), \(3\), \(4\) (\(\rho_{4}\oplus\rho_{4}^{\ast}\)), and \(4\) (the realification of \(\rho_{5}\), i.e. left multiplication on \(\mathbb{H}\cong\mathbb{R}^{4}\)).

## The two representations QGA already uses

These are not extra structure. They are left multiplication and conjugation.

**Defining spinor \(\rho_{5}\subset\mathrm{SU}(2)\).**  
Write \(q=z_{1}+z_{2} j\). Left multiplication by a unit is \(\mathbb{C}\)-linear on \((z_{1},z_{2})\) and lands in \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\). Restricted to \(2T\) this is \(\rho_{5}\). QGA’s Hopf map is constant on the left \(U(1)\) generated by \(i\), and

\[
2T\cap\mathrm{span}\{1,i\}=\{\pm 1,\pm i\}\cong C_{4}
\]

is the only piece of \(2T\) that stays in a single fiber of that \(h\). The companion field \(\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{O}}(q)=iq\) is the infinitesimal generator of this \(C_{4}\subset\rho_{5}\).

The 24 units occupy 6 octahedral points of \(S^{2}\), each a left \(C_{4}\)-orbit
\[
\{q,\,iq,\,-q,\,-iq\}.
\]

**Vector representation \(\rho_{3}\).**  
Conjugation \(v\mapsto qv\bar q\) on pure imaginaries. Kernel \(\{\pm 1\}\), image the 12-element tetrahedral group \(A_{4}\subset SO(3)\). This is Hatcher's “rotation” side and the attitude-kinematics side. It is not the Hopf fiber.

Left multiplication on the set \(\Lambda_{0}\) itself is the left regular representation, which decomposes as the sum of every irrep with multiplicity equal to its dimension:

\[
\mathbb{C}[2T]
\;\cong\;
\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{2}\oplus\rho_{2}^{\ast}\oplus 3\,\rho_{3}\oplus 2\,\rho_{4}\oplus 2\,\rho_{4}^{\ast}\oplus 2\,\rho_{5}.
\]

As a real permutation representation on 24 points it is more rigid: it is the Cayley embedding of \(2T\) into \(S_{24}\).

## McKay: why the dimensions are \(1,1,1,2,2,2,3\)

Tensor the defining spinor with each irrep and decompose. The resulting adjacency graph on the seven irreps is the affine Dynkin diagram \(\widetilde{E}_{6}\):

```
        ρ2
        |
ρ4* —— ρ5 —— ρ3 —— ρ5 —— ρ4
        |
       ρ2*
```

(with \(\rho_{1}\) the affine extending node, in the usual McKay labeling). That is the binary-tetrahedral case of McKay’s ADE correspondence; binary octahedral / icosahedral give \(\widetilde{E}_{7},\widetilde{E}_{8}\).

Dimensions of the nontrivial irreps are the dual Coxeter labels of \(E_{6}\): \(1,2,3,2,1,2\). This is a theorem about \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) finite subgroups, not a QGA Model.

The Kleinian singularity \(\mathbb{C}^{2}/2T\) is the \(E_{6}\) surface \(x^{2}+y^{3}+z^{4}=0\) (up to coordinate change). Resolving it reproduces the same diagram on exceptional curves.

Do not put McKay, Kleinian singularities, or character tables into the companion note or into \(Q\).

## What this does — and does not — give QGA

**Already a theorem, usable as input**

- \(2T\) acts on the left on \(\Lambda_{0}\) and on \((z_{1},z_{2})\).
- \(2T\) acts by conjugation on the Hopf base; the 24 units occupy 6 octahedral points of \(S^{2}\), each fiber a left \(C_{4}\)-orbit.
- \(\rho_{3}\) is the rotation action on that octahedron / on \(\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}\).
- Characters detect which proposed “adjacency” can be \(2T\)-invariant: an edge type must be a union of conjugacy-twisted pairs.

**Not a representation-theoretic free lunch**

- Irreps classify linear actions, not graphs. A \(2T\)-invariant subset of \(\Lambda_{0}\times\Lambda_{0}\) is a Model for \(E_{\parallel}\cup E_{\perp}\); invariance is the cheap half of OP1. Farey reduction \(\lvert ad-bc\rvert=1\) is a different condition.
- Right multiplication is the right regular representation. It commutes with left multiplication (Peter–Weyl), and for this \(h\) it does not preserve fibers unless the unit lies in \(\mathrm{span}\{1,i\}\). So “left-equivariant hence right-equivariant” is false as a representation statement.
- \(\rho_{2},\rho_{2}^{\ast}\) are the characters of \(2T/Q_{8}\cong C_{3}\). They know about the 3-cycle \(i\to j\to k\), not about mediants.
- Nothing here produces a mediant \(\oplus\) or an embedding \(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}\hookrightarrow\Lambda\).

## Cut for OP1

Classify \(2T\)-invariant directed graphs on 24 vertices whose along-fiber part is exactly the twelve edges of the six left \(C_{4}\)-cycles \(\{q,iq,-q,-iq\}\), then test Farey specialisation on one complex line.

Representation theory tells you the invariant graphs exist and how many orbits they come in. It does not pick Hatcher's determinant.

Allowed: \(2T\) as discrete gauge input. Forbidden: “\(2T\) therefore Farey.”
