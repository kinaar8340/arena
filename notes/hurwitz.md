# Hurwitz geometry: 24-cell and \(D_4\)

Hurwitz geometry is the 24-cell sitting as a lattice in \(\mathbb{H}\cong\mathbb{R}^{4}\). That is already theorem. The Farey reading is not.

Group and irreps: `notes/2T.md`. Incidence: `notes/op1.md`. Fiber of this \(h\): `notes/hopf.md`. QGA: Ch. 1 §1.4, Ch. 3 \(\Lambda_{0}\). Stereographic pole: Ch. 2, \(x_{4}=1\).

Claim label: **Theorem** for the lattice, the 24-cell, Euclidean algorithm, and \(h(\Lambda_{0})\). Declaring 24-cell edges to be \(E_{\parallel}\) or \(E_{\perp}\) is **Model** / OP1.

## The lattice

Hurwitz order
\[
\mathcal{H}
=
\mathbb{Z}[i,j,k]
\;\cup\;
\bigl(\tfrac12(1+i+j+k)+\mathbb{Z}[i,j,k]\bigr)
\subset\mathbb{H}.
\]
As a real lattice it is rank 4, generated by \(1,i,j,\omega\) with \(\omega=\tfrac12(1+i+j+k)\) (an order-6 unit; the \(C_{3}\) generator in `notes/2T.md` is \(-\omega\)). After a linear change of coordinates it is the root lattice \(D_{4}\):

\[
D_{4}=\bigl\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}:\textstyle\sum x_{i}\text{ even}\bigr\}.
\]

Lipschitz order \(L=\mathbb{Z}[i,j,k]\cong\mathbb{Z}^{4}\) is the coarser cubic lattice. \(\mathcal{H}\) is \(L\) plus the body-center of each odd-parity cube. Minimal vectors:

| Vector type | Count | Norm \(N(q)=q\bar q\) | Euclidean length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| units \(\Lambda_{0}\) | 24 | 1 | 1 |
| next layer | 24 | 2 | \(\sqrt{2}\) |

The 24 units are the vertices of a regular 24-cell. The 24 norm-2 points are a second 24-cell, dual and concentric, scaled by \(\sqrt{2}\). That is why the 24-cell is self-dual: vertices of one are cell-centers of the other.

Left or right multiplication by a unit is a lattice automorphism of \(\mathcal{H}\). The full unit group is \(2T\), order 24 (`notes/2T.md`). The orthogonal group of the lattice is larger (\(\mathrm{Aut}(D_{4})\cong W(F_{4})\), order 1152); QGA uses only the multiplicative units.

## The 24-cell

Regular convex 4-polytope \(\{3,4,3\}\). Schläfli: tetrahedral vertex figure, octahedral cells.

| Element | Number |
| --- | --- |
| vertices | 24 |
| edges | 96 |
| triangular faces | 96 |
| octahedral cells | 24 |

Each vertex has 8 neighbors (degree \(8\); \(24\times 8/2=96\) edges). Distances from \(1\) among the units:

| Points | Count | \(N(1-q)\) | Length | 24-cell role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(-1\) | 1 | 4 | \(2\) | opposite vertex, not an edge |
| \(\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\) | 6 | 2 | \(\sqrt{2}\) | face / cell diagonals |
| \(\tfrac12(1\pm i\pm j\pm k)\) | 8 | 1 | \(1\) | **edges** (inner product \(1/2\)) |
| \(\tfrac12(-1\pm i\pm j\pm k)\) | 8 | 3 | \(\sqrt{3}\) | not edges |

The 24-cell’s edges are the length-1 pairs: each unit is joined to the eight half-integer units of inner product \(1/2\). From \(1\), those neighbors are \(\tfrac12(1\pm i\pm j\pm k)\).

The eight Lipschitz units \(\{\pm 1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}\) form a 16-cell (cross-polytope) inscribed in the 24-cell. The sixteen half-integer units form a tesseract, the dual of that 16-cell. The 24-cell is the convex hull of both.

Kissing picture in \(\mathbb{R}^{4}\): 24 non-overlapping balls of radius \(1/2\) about the units all touch the ball about \(0\). That is the \(D_{4}\) kissing arrangement (kissing number 24 in 4D).

## Hopf picture of the same 24 points

QGA’s map \(h:S^{3}\to S^{2}\) is constant on left \(U(1)\). Intersection

\[
2T\cap\mathrm{span}\{1,i\}=\{\pm 1,\pm i\}\cong C_{4},
\]

so the 24 units occupy six fibers, four points each. The six base points are the regular octahedron:

\[
h(1)=(0,0,1),\quad
h(j)=(0,0,-1),
\]
and four equatorial points from the half-integer orbits.

Geometry of one fiber through \(1\):

\[
\{1,\,i,\,-1,\,-i\}
\]

is a square in the \(\{1,i\}\)-plane. It is **not** a 24-cell edge cycle: \(1\)–\(i\) is a length-\(\sqrt{2}\) diagonal, not an edge. So the structure-group neighbors on \(\Lambda_{0}\) and the 24-cell 1-skeleton are **different graphs on the same 24 vertices**.

That is the first geometric reason \(E_{\parallel}\) and \(E_{\perp}\) must be chosen separately (OP1). The 24-cell edges mix fibers; the Hopf \(C_{4}\) stays inside a fiber and skips 24-cell edges.

Cells: each octahedral 3-cell of the 24-cell projects under \(h\) to a spherical triangle or to a degenerate set, depending on how many of its six vertices share a fiber. That is a finite, checkable table — useful for an OP1 candidate, not a theorem that the 24-cell is Farey.

## Euclidean algorithm (why people say “Farey”)

\(\mathcal{H}\) is a left and right Euclidean domain for the norm \(N\):

\[
\forall a,b\neq 0\;\exists\, q,r:\quad
a=bq+r,\qquad N(r)<N(b).
\]

Proof is geometric: the Voronoi cell of \(0\) in \(\mathcal{H}\) is a 24-cell of circumradius \(1\) (any quaternion is within distance \(<1\) of some Hurwitz integer; the worst points are 16-cell centers at distance \(\sqrt{2}/2<1\)). Divide, round to nearest lattice point, remainder strictly smaller.

Consequences that are theorems:

- every left (right) ideal is principal;
- primes factor with uniqueness up to units;
- \(N(q)=p\) prime in \(\mathbb{Z}\) iff \(q\) is prime in \(\mathcal{H}\) (except the ramified prime \(2=-i(1+i)^{2}\) up to units).

Classical Farey adjacency \(\lvert ad-bc\rvert=1\) is the 2-dimensional shadow of “two unimodular vectors form a basis of \(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\).” The Hurwitz analogue people want is: two elements form part of a left \(\mathcal{H}\)-basis, i.e. the Dieudonné / reduced-norm minor is a unit. That lift is exactly OP1, because:

- noncommutativity (left basis \(\neq\) right basis);
- the extra \(S^{1}\) fiber has no \(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\) analogue;
- embedding \(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})\) into \(\mathcal{H}\) or into \(h(\mathcal{H})\) is a choice.

The Euclidean property gives an algorithm. It does not name the graph.

## Two radii, two polytopes, two groups

Do not collapse these:

| Object | Lives in | Symmetry | QGA role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 24-cell vertices | \(S^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}\) | \(W(F_{4})\) / \(2T\) multiplicative | \(\Lambda_{0}\) |
| inscribed 16-cell | 8 Lipschitz units | \(Q_{8}\) | even sublattice |
| dual tesseract | 16 half-integer units | — | odd coset |
| Hopf image | octahedron in \(S^{2}\) | \(A_{4}\) via \(\rho_{3}\) | 6 base points |
| geodesic icosahedron \(\nu=16\) | \(S^{2}\), 2562 verts / 5120 faces | \(A_{5}\) / \(2I\) | a different base mesh |
| \(2O\) 48 points | \(S^{3}\), not a lattice | binary octahedral | denser fiber \(C_{8}\), leaves \(\mathcal{H}\) |

The 24-cell is self-dual and has no icosahedral cells. An \(m=16\) geodesic grid is a triangulation of the base, with icosahedral symmetry. Mixing the two is how “5120 faces” quietly replaces Hurwitz by \(2I\).

## What stereographic does to this

The book helper stereographs from \(x_{4}=1\) (Ch. 2). In QGA coordinates \(q=x_{1}+x_{2}i+x_{3}j+x_{4}k\), that pole is \(k\), a Hurwitz unit.

- The fiber through \(k\), \(\{k,ik,-k,-ik\}=\{\pm j,\pm k\}\), meets the pole: stereographic sends that circle to a line.
- The fiber through \(1\), \(\{\pm 1,\pm i\}\), does not meet the pole: it becomes a circle in \(\mathbb{R}^{3}\).
- Every other Hopf fiber becomes a circle in \(\mathbb{R}^{3}\).
- 24-cell edges become circular arcs (stereographic preserves circles/spheres).

Linked circles in a loom or GPU view are this picture of **fibers**, not of 24-cell edges. Drawing the 24-cell 1-skeleton in the same \(\mathbb{R}^{3}\) is a different wireframe. Both are legitimate illustrations; they answer different incidence questions.

## Geometry that is safe to use as input

**Theorem-level**

- \(\mathcal{H}\) is \(D_{4}\), Euclidean, 24 units, 24-cell.
- Left/right unit multiplication preserves \(\mathcal{H}\) and permutes \(\Lambda_{0}\).
- \(h(\Lambda_{0})\) is 6 octahedral points; fibers through units are left \(C_{4}\).
- 24-cell 1-skeleton \(\neq\) Hopf-fiber graph on the same vertices.
- Nearest-lattice rounding is the Euclidean algorithm.

**Still Model / OP1**

- Declaring 24-cell edges to be \(E_{\perp}\) or \(E_{\parallel}\).
- Declaring left-associated pairs \(\{q,uq\}\) to be Farey neighbors.
- Any mediant built from Hurwitz addition (not well-defined on fibers).
- Using the 24-cell as Hatcher's modular diagram.

## Two readings

One stays inside \(\mathcal{H}\). The other quietly changes the vertex set. Only the first is a couple of moves ahead on the locked cut; the second is a new Model.

### Reading A — six planes, same 24 vertices

On \(\mathbb{H}\cong\mathbb{R}^{4}\) the three imaginary units give three orthogonal splittings

\[
\mathbb{R}^{4}
=
\mathrm{span}\{1,i\}\oplus\mathrm{span}\{j,k\}
=
\mathrm{span}\{1,j\}\oplus\mathrm{span}\{k,i\}
=
\mathrm{span}\{1,k\}\oplus\mathrm{span}\{i,j\}.
\]

Label the planes

\[
\begin{align*}
i &\colon \mathrm{span}\{1,i\},
&
i^{\ast} &\colon \mathrm{span}\{j,k\},\\
j &\colon \mathrm{span}\{1,j\},
&
j^{\ast} &\colon \mathrm{span}\{k,i\},\\
k &\colon \mathrm{span}\{1,k\},
&
k^{\ast} &\colon \mathrm{span}\{i,j\}.
\end{align*}
\]

No new points. Algebraically \(i=jk\); the star is the **orthogonal 2-plane**, not a new unit.

Left (resp. right) multiplication by \(i\) is the isoclinic pair of \(90^{\circ}\) rotations in \(i\) and \(i^{\ast}\) (opposite relative sense for left vs right). That pair *is* QGA’s Hopf generator \(q\mapsto e^{i\theta}q\) and the other circle \(q\mapsto qi\). Same for \(j,j^{\ast}\) and \(k,k^{\ast}\): three Hopf maps, three structure-group circles, three complementary circles.

On \(\Lambda_{0}\) each plane cuts out a \(C_{4}\):

| Plane | Square through \(1\) (or through \(j\) for \(i^{\ast}\)) | Lengths |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(i\) | \(\{1,i,-1,-i\}\) | \(\sqrt{2}\) diagonals |
| \(j\) | \(\{1,j,-1,-j\}\) | \(\sqrt{2}\) |
| \(k\) | \(\{1,k,-1,-k\}\) | \(\sqrt{2}\) |
| \(i^{\ast}\) | \(\{j,k,-j,-k\}\) | \(\sqrt{2}\) |
| \(j^{\ast}\) | \(\{k,i,-k,-i\}\) | \(\sqrt{2}\) |
| \(k^{\ast}\) | \(\{i,j,-i,-j\}\) | \(\sqrt{2}\) |

These six squares are the Lipschitz \(Q_{8}\) Cayley graph, drawn as three coordinate Hopf cycles plus three complementary cycles. None of them is a 24-cell edge. From \(1\), the eight 24-cell edges go only to \(\tfrac12(1\pm i\pm j\pm k)\), off every coordinate plane. The eight points \(\tfrac12(-1\pm i\pm j\pm k)\) sit at length \(\sqrt{3}\) and are not edges.

So the three-graph comparison already locked just acquired names:

| Graph | Name in this reading |
| --- | --- |
| Hopf \(C_{4}\) of QGA’s \(h\) | the \(i\)-square |
| rest of the \(Q_{8}\) graph | the other five squares \(j,k,i^{\ast},j^{\ast},k^{\ast}\) |
| 24-cell edges | the only edges that leave the six planes (96 undirected) |

If you want a fiber-aware adjacency, \(E_{\parallel}\) is a union of some of these six \(C_{4}\)s, and \(E_{\perp}\) has to be allowed to use the half-integer edges that the planes do not contain. That does **not** identify \(E_{\parallel}\) with 24-cell edges.

QGA’s \(h\) picked the \(i\)-plane. The other five squares are symmetries of the stage, not of that bundle. Do not write \(E_{\parallel}=\) all six squares.

Farey restriction is now a list, not one line: \(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})\) can be tried in \(i\), in \(j\), or in \(k\) (Gaussian integers \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\), \(\mathbb{Z}[j]\), \(\mathbb{Z}[k]\)). The starred planes are not unital copies of \(\mathbb{C}\) through \(1\), so they are the wrong place to embed \(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}\) if you want \(1\) as \(\infty\) or \(0\). Exercise 3.H becomes: Farey in each unital plane, report the three scores, do not average them.

Left vs right is visible per plane: left \(U(1)_{i}\) preserves the \(i\)-fibers of QGA’s \(h\); right \(U(1)_{i}\) preserves the other fibration and rotates \(i^{\ast}\). Equivariance of a candidate graph must be checked against six 1-parameter groups, not one. That is Exercise 4.H with the right list of gauges.

Still OP1: none of this produces \(\lvert ad-bc\rvert=1\) or a mediant. It only stops you from treating “neighbors of \(1\)” as a single undirected blob.

### Reading B — extra rays (\(i^{\ast}\) as a new vertex)

The other reading adds points on the same six planes, typically

\[
\frac{1\pm i}{\sqrt{2}},\ 
\frac{1\pm j}{\sqrt{2}},\ 
\frac{1\pm k}{\sqrt{2}},\ 
\frac{j\pm k}{\sqrt{2}},\ 
\frac{k\pm i}{\sqrt{2}},\ 
\frac{i\pm j}{\sqrt{2}}.
\]

That is 24 extra unit vectors: the norm-\(2\) Hurwitz integers projected onto \(S^{3}\). Union with \(\Lambda_{0}\) is \(2O\), 48 points, not a subset of \(\mathcal{H}\). The same 24 directions unnormalized are the second 24-cell (norm \(2\)) and, together with \(\Lambda_{0}\), the \(F_{4}\) root system. \(W(F_{4})\) then looks tempting as a gauge group. QGA already refused that group.

Do not take this move on the locked lattice cut. It changes the vertex set, leaves the order, and replaces \(2T\) by \(2O\) or \(W(F_{4})\). Allowed later as a named Model (“\(F_{4}\)-rays” or “binary octahedral sample”). Forbidden as a silent upgrade of \(\Lambda_{0}\).

## Couple of moves ahead, still on 24 vertices

A small matrix on 24 vertices: six labeled \(C_{4}\)s, plus 96 undirected 24-cell edges.

1. Mark every pair with a tag in \(\{i,j,k,i^{\ast},j^{\ast},k^{\ast},\omega\}\) where \(\omega\) means “half-integer / 24-cell edge.”
2. Left multiply by \(i,j,k\). See which tags are permuted. (They will be: \(2T\) permutes the three unital planes as \(A_{4}\) permutes axes, and permutes the three starred planes the same way.)
3. Right multiply by \(i,j,k\). Same permutation of labels, opposite action on fibers.
4. Restrict to \(\mathrm{span}\{1,i\}\cap\Lambda_{0}=\{1,i,-1,-i\}\). The only surviving tagged edges are the \(i\)-square. Classical Farey on that \(\mathbb{P}^{1}\) wants \(\{0,1,\infty\}\) and mediants, which this \(C_{4}\) does not give you — four roots of unity, not \(\mathbb{Q}\). That negative is the Exercise 3.H report, not a bug.
5. To get rationals you have to leave the unit sphere: take \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\subset\mathrm{span}\{1,i\}\), not \(\Lambda_{0}\cap\mathrm{span}\{1,i\}\). Then you are doing Gaussian Farey (`notes/gaussian_farey.md`) in one plane and asking whether half-integer edges supply the missing “other prime.” Score missed unit-det pairs and extra edges of det-norm \(>1\). That is the first honest bridge toward a left-basis predicate, and it already uses points of norm \(>1\). Still OP1, but now the extra \(S^{1}\) and the embedding choice are explicit.

## What not to do

- Do not put \(i,i^{\ast},j,j^{\ast},k,k^{\ast}\) into the companion generator. That would be six fiber fields, i.e. a third lobe.
- Do not write \(E_{\parallel}=\) all six squares. QGA’s \(h\) picked one plane.
- Do not call the six-plane coloring a quaternionic Farey diagram.
- Do not identify \(i^{\ast}\) with a new Hurwitz unit.
- Do not silently add the 24 rays of Reading B.

Allowed: six named 2-planes as labels on the three locked graphs. Forbidden: extra vertices, extra group, or “augmented orthogonal = solved adjacency.”

Allowed: 24-cell as the geometry of \(\Lambda_{0}\). Forbidden: “the 24-cell is Farey.”
